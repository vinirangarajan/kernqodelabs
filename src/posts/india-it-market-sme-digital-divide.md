## India's IT Numbers Are Impressive

India's IT and tech services industry crossed **$250 billion in annual revenue** in 2025, according to NASSCOM. With over 5 million direct employees and exports powering a huge chunk of India's economy, the sector is undeniably thriving.

The big names — TCS, Infosys, Wipro, HCL — are delivering complex enterprise software to Fortune 500 companies across the globe. Indian engineers are building the systems that power banks, airlines, and hospitals worldwide.

Great story. But there's a side of this story that rarely gets told.

## The Digital Divide Inside India

Here's the contradiction: while Indian companies build world-class software for global clients, **millions of Indian businesses have almost no digital infrastructure at all.**

Walk into a kirana store in Coimbatore, a textiles shop in Erode, or a logistics company in Salem. Their "software" is often:

- WhatsApp groups for orders and customer communication
- Excel sheets (or notebooks) for inventory
- Manual invoicing with no follow-up system
- Zero visibility into cash flow

These aren't failing businesses. Many of them are profitable and have been operating for decades. But they're operating at a fraction of their potential — because no one has built the right tools *for them*, at the right price.

## Why Big Tech Doesn't Solve This

Enterprise software like SAP, Salesforce, or even Zoho is built for companies with dedicated IT teams and training budgets. A grocery store owner in Tamil Nadu can't afford a ₹50,000/year subscription and a 3-month implementation project.

The mobile-first SaaS boom helped — apps like Vyapar, KhataBook, and OkCredit made basic bookkeeping accessible. But there's still a massive gap in the middle: businesses that have outgrown WhatsApp but can't afford enterprise tools.

- **ONDC (Open Network for Digital Commerce)** — India's open e-commerce network is a massive opportunity to bring SMBs online without depending on Amazon or Flipkart
- **UPI for Business** — real-time payments are already solved; the next layer is smart financial tooling on top
